# Apple Mail responsiveness — 1.8.1

The previous scanner could traverse years of Mail continuously, rereading unrelated bodies and repeatedly evaluating date-filtered message references for each field. On the user's machine, stopping that reader reduced Mail from about 32% CPU to idle.

Apple Mail ingestion now uses resumable pages: at most eight body reads and twelve mailbox queries per page, a four-second cooperative page budget, a ten-second owned-process deadline, and no more than two pages per invocation with a twelve-second overall read budget shared by page deadlines (plus process cleanup). An individual synchronous Apple event can exceed the cooperative budget until its process deadline; subsequent readers inherit only the invocation time remaining. Automatic invocations wait at least five minutes after completion; manual invocations enforce fifteen seconds of rest. History windows begin at seven days and narrow on timeout or stalled metadata queries. Failed windows defer to a later invocation rather than immediately retrying. Progress is committed atomically with saved correspondence. Checkpoints never advance past unfinished windows.

Stable Mail-local IDs avoid repeatedly resolving a date search for each message field. Read-only metadata caches record completed reads, including unrelated messages, without storing unrelated bodies. Completed folders and partial date windows resume across restarts. A company-profile change uses a new matching scope; account caches are isolated. Empty bodies remain eligible for delayed recovery, including messages not yet known to match a company. Mailbox-date discovery itself is paged and resumable. Inaccessible metadata never falls back to a whole-history scan.

Scanning defers while Mail is frontmost and rechecks between body reads. Pause scan disables automatic scanning, cancels the owned reader and preserves the unfinished checkpoint. Turning off automatic scanning also cancels an active reader; queued-worker generation checks close the pause/start race. Backend shutdown terminates its owned Mail reader. Provider API ingestion also checks cancellation before committing a fetched page.

Validation: 241 Python tests and 80 JavaScript tests, including a 300-message paged inbox, unrelated-message caching, deletion/reordering, selected account/spam/archive handling, malformed pages, atomic rollback, restart progress, body recovery, metadata inventory, foreground and mid-batch foreground deferral, cancellation, cooldowns, timeout splitting and stalled-window narrowing. HTTP tests verify pause persistence. Packaged smoke checks verify scanner controls, interval labels, app version and the desktop mail bridge. Fresh restore tests additionally exposed and fixed a pre-existing rejection of the new job_ad category during backup restoration.

Real Mail testing used a private consistent database copy, with no classification worker or approval operations. A timing trace reduced an individual message-field read from roughly 6.1 seconds to 0.13 seconds by using stable IDs. A three-page sample took 5.96, 5.01 and 4.18 seconds and used 5.22 Mail CPU-seconds over 45.25 wall-seconds including rest. It exercised metadata progress and new-body progress without rereading complete cached bodies. Real-reader cancellation finished within three seconds, reaped the child and left the unfinished checkpoint unchanged. Live applications and review decisions were not used as test mutations.

These measurements are representative, not a guarantee of zero CPU activity or an upper bound on Mail's own internal work. Apple events can be slow on large or busy mailboxes. Foreground deferral, strict process deadlines and long idle intervals prevent sustained scanning; a large backlog will take longer to collect. Direct Gmail/Outlook/IMAP connections bypass Apple Mail entirely and remain available.
